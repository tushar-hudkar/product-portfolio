### Business constraints

- **High System Latency:** Generating a complete listing required multiple AI models, validation checks, and data sources to work together, increasing processing time and delaying content delivery.
- **No real-time collaboration:** There was no real-time feedback or chat system, which slowed communication and decision-making.
- **Lack of Performance Visibility:** Sellers cannot predict listing performance before publishing, making optimization largely trial-and-error.

## Research Insights

### Market Research

The market is large and presents multiple opportunities for observation and analysis.

More than 60% of sales on Amazon come from third-party independent sellers, the majority of whom are small and medium-sized enterprises/businesses (SMEs/SMBs).

### SME Branding Maturity Levels

- **Unstructured:** No formal brand guidelines; listings are generic and inconsistent.
- **Basic Branding:** Use of logos and labels; products are listed with standard content and basic A+ templates.
- **Structured:** Clear brand guidelines are followed, with the use of customized A+ modules and consistent visual identity.
- **Data-Driven:** In addition to strong branding, sellers leverage analytics and AI to ensure consistency and deliver personalized A+ content.

### Competition research

1. **No Unified Platform:** There is no single platform capable of generating a complete (at least basic) product listing in one place.
2. **Lack of eCommerce Focus:** Most tools primarily focus on social media images, lifestyle and model imagery rather than end-to-end eCommerce listing needs.
3. **No Amazon Integration:** There is no direct integration with Amazon seller accounts, resulting in reliance on manual processes.
4. **No Listing Performance Analysis:** Existing solutions lack a ranking or analytics system to evaluate listing performance.
5. **High Cost and Inefficiency:** Pricing is often high, making these tools less accessible for small sellers, and the overall process remains time-consuming.

## Opportunities and gaps

### How do sellers manage digital assets?

1. Build an in-house team or hire an agency to get things done.
2. Sellers follow separate, custom workflows for each asset type.
   - A+ content creation is handled as a completely separate process.
   - Hire professional photographers and models for lifestyle and on-model shoots.
3. Assets are managed using folder systems and CSV formats across cloud storage or internal tools, with uploading and updates handled manually.
4. Updating an existing listing requires repeating much of the process from scratch, rather than building on existing assets.
5. Sellers rely heavily on Amazon's native analytics, with limited ability to derive deeper insights or optimize listings proactively.

### Why do they operate this way?

1. Sellers prioritize managing their Amazon Seller Central accounts, including compliance, fulfillment, and payments.
2. While managing large inventories, sellers focus heavily on running operations efficiently.
3. Driving sales is the priority, while branding often comes second.
4. Sellers understand their products well but don't have enough time for competitor research.
5. Many sellers do not come from design or technical backgrounds.

### How should it be?

1. Ensure consistency in branding and communication to build trust, convey value, and improve discoverability.
2. Develop a clear understanding of the target segment (including demographics and cultural context) and the market.
3. Follow Amazon image guidelines, including size, background color, and margin requirements.

### Hypothesis

1. **New sellers (who need branding support):** This is the ideal stage to onboard them with a fresh approach and clear context, as they are just beginning their journey on Amazon.
2. **Established brands (already listed on Amazon):** These sellers have access to significant data, making it easier to understand their business and optimize listings. However, if they are already using another solution, onboarding them may require additional effort.

### Key features

1. Integrate with **Amazon Seller Central** to import catalog and push updates back seamlessly.
2. A **score mechanism** as a Product Listing Analyzer that evaluates listing performance as a ranking or score.
3. **Actionable insights** that allow users to **customise** listing components themselves as needed.
4. **Personalised recommendations** through **brand integration**.
5. Users can submit listings for **"Pro-Touch"** updates, and easily provide instructions for required changes.

### Score Mechanism

Evaluating the readiness of product listings is challenging, as effective listing creation involves multiple nuanced factors — white BG images, lifestyle images, on-model imagery, comparison & size charts, product descriptions and A+ content (basic and premium), each with its own score weightage.

An effective product page involves many nuances, including detailed A+ content, high-engagement infographics, and comprehensive product descriptions. A scoring mechanism simplifies listing evaluation and helps measure success.
