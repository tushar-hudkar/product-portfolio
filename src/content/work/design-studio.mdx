## Existing workflow

> Client → Sales/Marketing → Requirement collection → Cloud storage → Internal team → Task delegation → Designer → Review → Delivery

A custom form was used for getting requirements and references, which were then passed to a dedicated team.

## Challenges

1. **Highly manual workflow:** Requirements, references, source assets, instructions, and feedback were distributed across different communication channels.
2. **Linear production workflows:** Existing productivity tools were optimized around sequential operations.
3. **Fragmented asset structure:** This structure worked for individual production activities, but did not represent how customers actually think about their product.
4. **Limited customer control.**
5. Simplify the technically complex AI workflow into a sequence of understandable decisions.

## Goal

The goal was not simply to build another image-editing tool. The larger opportunity was to create a product that connected customer intent, creative production, task management, and final asset delivery into one experience.

1. **Reuse existing product assets:** Allow users to create on-model imagery from product-only images or mannequin photography, preserving the value of their existing content.
2. **Expand creative possibilities:** Enable users to adapt imagery for different demographic audiences and choose suitable AI-generated models and viewing angles.
3. **Make AI generation controllable:** Provide visual references, mask adjustments, editing tools, and generation settings so users can guide the output rather than depend entirely on a prompt.
4. **Connect mobile capture with web-based creation:** Enable users to capture product images through the mobile app and use those assets in the broader image-generation workflow.
5. **Improve product and business performance:** Design the experience to increase feature adoption, reduce time to value, and improve the percentage of users who successfully complete image-generation tasks.

## Key Design Decisions

### From asset-centric to product-centric

Instead of designing the system around individual image types, I organized the experience around the product and its complete asset requirements.

I changed the conceptual model from *"folders of asset types"* to *"products with multiple asset requirements."*

### From linear workflow to flexible workflow

The system needed to support both self-service and managed production rather than forcing every user through the same process.

### From feature delivery to measurable behavior

I defined success using adoption, time to value, completion, and operational metrics instead of treating feature launch as the final outcome.

## Design Principles

I established a few principles to guide the experience.

<Cards>
  <Card title="Product-first, not asset-first">The primary unit of work should be the product, not the individual image.</Card>
  <Card title="Self-service where possible, managed service where necessary">Users should be able to complete simple tasks themselves while still having an easy path to request professional assistance.</Card>
  <Card title="Reduce context switching">Requirements, source images, instructions, editing tools, production status, and output should live within the same workflow wherever possible.</Card>
  <Card title="Make progress visible and design for scale">The interface needed to work for a single product as well as large-scale e-commerce workflows involving hundreds or thousands of assets.</Card>
</Cards>

## Interface outlook

Instead of forcing users to move between multiple tools, the experience brought common image-production activities into one web-based workflow.

1. **Marketplace-Aware Design:** One of the important product opportunities was that the same product often needed different assets depending on where it was being sold.
2. **Transform product images into on-model imagery:** Users can begin with an existing product image, including apparel displayed on a mannequin.
3. **Reference-based generation:** Users can browse reference images, search for suitable examples, upload their own references where supported, or use a manual prompt.
4. The interface was designed to reflect how the e-commerce assets related to one specific product should be organized, rather than how an internal production team historically organized folders.
5. Self-service would not solve every creative requirement. For complex requirements, users could submit a project to the internal production team.
